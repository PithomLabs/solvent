No replanning.

---

# 2. Source Fidelity (Highest Priority)

The attached **Project Gutenberg edition of *The Wandering Jew*** is the canonical source.

Every page must:

* follow the novel in sequence
* never invent scenes
* never move events out of order
* never skip important story beats without deliberate editorial justification
* distinguish clearly between adaptation and invention

Whenever practical:

* quote directly from the novel
* shorten only when necessary for comic readability

---

# 3. Editorial Review Before Every "next"

Before planning a page, always perform an editorial review.

## Look Back

Ask:

* What dramatic beat did the previous page complete?
* Is the previous page already a satisfying conclusion?
* Did the previous page already resolve the current scene?

---

## Look at Current Position

Ask:

* Does the remaining material in this chapter justify another page?
* Or would another page merely stretch dialogue?

---

## Look Ahead

Ask:

* Where does Eugène Sue naturally transition?
* Would ending the chapter here create stronger pacing?
* Would opening the next chapter instead produce a better page turn?

Then decide whether the next page should:

* continue the chapter

or

* conclude the chapter and begin the next.

Never assume continuation automatically.

---

# 4. Chapter Pacing

Do **not** artificially prolong chapters.

Every page must justify its existence.

Some chapters deserve:

* one page

Others deserve:

* many pages

depending entirely on dramatic importance.

The comic should read like a premium graphic novel—not a literal transcription.

---

# 5. One Dramatic Beat Per Page

Each page should revolve around one meaningful dramatic movement.

Examples:

* reveal
* decision
* emotional reversal
* discovery
* confrontation
* transition
* climax

Avoid pages that merely move characters from one paragraph to the next.

---

# 6. Visual Storytelling First

Artwork carries the story.

Prefer:

* expressions
* body language
* lighting
* composition
* environment

over excessive narration.

Use text only where necessary.

---

# 7. Typography (Locked)

Always prioritize readability.

Rules:

* large lettering
* generous margins
* concise captions
* concise dialogue
* avoid cramped balloons
* remove redundant text already shown elsewhere (for example, chapter titles repeated inside Panel 1)

If a quotation is too long:

shorten it without changing its meaning.

---

# 8. Panel Composition

Use cinematic variety.

Possible layouts:

* establishing shot
* close-up
* medium shot
* panoramic panel
* splash panel
* symbolic composition

Merge panels when it improves storytelling or prevents distortion.

Never shrink important artwork simply to maintain an arbitrary panel count.

---

# 9. Character Continuity

Maintain absolute consistency across pages.

Characters must retain:

* facial features
* hairstyles
* clothing
* age
* posture
* handedness
* recurring expressions

Objects must remain consistent:

* portraits
* rooms
* furniture
* props
* lighting
* architecture

Example:

Marius now consistently writes with the **right hand**.

Do not switch unless the story explicitly requires it.

---

# 10. Historical Authenticity

Everything should reflect:

* 17th-century flashbacks
* 19th-century present
* architecture
* costumes
* furniture
* legal documents
* religious imagery

Avoid modern visual cues.

---

# 11. Image Style (Locked)

Every page should use:

* 1536 × 1024 landscape
* cinematic historical realism
* oil-painting texture
* premium graphic novel quality
* highly detailed environments
* consistent recurring characters
* readable typography
* correctly pointed speech balloons
* no duplicated dialogue
* no duplicated panels
* no distorted recurring faces

---

# 12. Adaptation Philosophy

We are **not** adapting paragraph-by-paragraph.

We are adapting **dramatic structure**.

That means:

* compress routine movement
* expand important revelations
* preserve Sue's intended pacing
* strengthen page turns
* never sacrifice the novel's meaning

The reader should experience the comic as though Eugène Sue himself had conceived it in graphic-novel form.

# Updated "next" Planning Algorithm (Locked)

Whenever you say **"next"**, I will not immediately plan the next page. Instead, I'll go through the following sequence.

## Step 0 — Coverage Ledger

Determine exactly where we are in the attached novel.

Identify:

* the exact material already adapted,
* the first unread sentence,
* the exact source coverage for the proposed page.

The page must begin at the first unread material.

Never continue merely because the theme is similar.

---

## Step 1 — Look Back

Review the immediately preceding comic page.

Evaluate:

* dramatic beat
* dialogue
* symbolism
* composition
* emotional movement

Ask:

> What has already been communicated?

The next page must **not** communicate the same thing again.

---

## Step 2 — Look Ahead

Read ahead in the attached novel.

Determine:

* where the current dramatic beat naturally ends,
* where the next dramatic beat begins,
* whether the chapter should continue,
* or whether ending the chapter would produce stronger pacing.

The goal is editorial coherence, not mechanical continuation.

---

## Step 3 — Justification (New Locked Requirement)

Every planned page must include a new section:

## **Why this page is necessary**

This justification must explicitly answer:

* Why is this page required?
* Why can this material not simply be merged with the previous page?
* Why should it not be postponed to the following page?
* What **new dramatic information** does the reader gain?
* What evidence from looking back and looking ahead supports this decision?

This is effectively a peer review of the plan before it is approved.

If I cannot justify the page, then the page should not exist.

---

## Step 4 — Duplicate Detection

Compare the proposed page against the previous page.

Check for duplication in:

* central dramatic idea
* visual symbolism
* emotional beat
* dialogue purpose
* composition

If substantial overlap exists, discard the plan and re-evaluate from the source.

---

## Step 5 — Page Test

Answer this question:

> **After reading this page, what can the reader now understand that they could not understand after the previous page?**

If the answer is weak, the page needs to be redesigned.

---

## Step 6 — Produce the Plan

Only after all previous steps pass will I produce:

* Title
* Dramatic Purpose
* **Why this page is necessary** (new mandatory section)
* Source Coverage (Locked)
* Panel-by-panel breakdown
* Faithful dialogue/captions
* End-of-page hook
* Editorial QA
